It started with a simple question: if a city is having a bad day — outages, closures, public-safety alerts — why do residents still have to stitch that story together across five apps?

## The idea

We wanted one dashboard that could answer: *what is happening in my city right now?* Not a polished municipal portal. A real-time civic intelligence surface powered by agents that gather, normalize, and explain events.

## Architecture

At a high level:

1. **Ingestion** — agents pull structured and unstructured civic signals.
2. **Orchestration** — a FastAPI layer coordinates agent work and stores state in Firestore.
3. **Presentation** — a dashboard surfaces prioritized alerts for residents and officials.

The hard part was not “call an LLM.” The hard part was keeping agent outputs grounded, ranked, and useful under time pressure.

## What I owned

I focused on **backend and agent orchestration** — defining how agents collaborated, how failures were handled, and how the API stayed fast enough for a live demo.

## What we learned

- Scope ruthlessly. A thin vertical slice beats a half-finished platform.
- Agent systems need guardrails early: timeouts, fallbacks, and clear ownership of each task.
- Judges respond to clarity. A readable story of problem → solution → demo mattered as much as the stack.

## Result

**2nd place** out of 34 teams and 194 participants at DevFest DC.
